# Actual publication dates and expanded interim coverage

Specification recorded before calculating revised portfolio returns, 3 November 2026.

## Authorized changes

1. Replace assumed financial-report availability lags with evidenced publication or filing dates. Every admitted financial observation must have all five required numerical inputs supported by a dated original issuer report or filing. Do not attach an original publication date to a revised vendor-history value.
2. Expand interim financial coverage from original statements. Construct trailing-twelve-month flows only from observed compatible reporting periods. Annual plus current year-to-date minus comparable prior year-to-date is permitted; no scaling of a quarter or half-year into an invented annual number. Retain half-year reporting frequency where that is all the issuer publishes.

## Inherited rules

Keep the same retrospective 31-company candidate universe, observed prices and FX, endpoint (2 November 2026), one-quarter normal entry/exit qualification, Q75 and Q50 entry variants, Q25 exit, 1-9 holdings, quarter-end normal membership decisions, monthly equal weighting plus the existing 1.5 percentage-point top-quartile tilt, and 10/15 basis-point US/EU trading costs. No momentum, volatility sizing, new leverage, new cash overlay, or parameter search.

The existing information cutoff remains the first day of the rebalance month. An observation is usable only if its complete availability date is strictly before that cutoff. Thus a report released during a month first becomes usable at a later modeled rebalance. Keep the inherited 550-day annual and 275-day interim freshness limits measured from the actual fiscal period end. Annual records retain the annual freshness limit even when they coincide with the fourth quarter.

When multiple statements are needed for a trailing calculation, availability is no earlier than the latest publication of its components. Prior comparisons in a later report become available on that later report's date, not retrospectively. Original data-access gaps and actual overdue reporting must be labeled separately where evidence allows; never fill missing values with zero or a neutral score.

## Experiments

Preserve the previous 1-9 results. Compare actual-date annual and expanded actual-date hybrid panels, their Q75/Q50 portfolios and equal-weight covered-universe controls. Where possible also retain a hybrid panel restricted to the original nine quarterly-covered names to distinguish coverage expansion from the availability-date and source changes. Report earliest feasible histories and matched-start histories separately. Any narrowed universe or infeasible interval must be explicit rather than disguised by cash or vendor fallback.

## Evidence and limitations

Original issuer numerical tables and dates must be retained alongside normalized inputs. Track sources, actual fiscal period ends, original versus later comparative figures, aggregation arithmetic, and exclusions. This financial-data revision alone cannot remove retrospective-universe bias or turn the reduced two-factor reconstruction into the paper's full model.

## Controlled timing comparison (recorded before the first revised return calculation)

Use the same original financial values with availability set to the later of actual publication and period end plus 180 days (annual) or 90 days (interim). This delayed control isolates earlier use of already-public information without fabricating earlier availability for reports that actually arrived late. It is not the old revised-vendor backtest. Run annual, original-nine hybrid and expanded hybrid under both timing treatments; use each region's latest feasible start across these experiments and the inherited main references as its matched start. Also show a common cross-region start and zero/double transaction-cost sensitivity for expanded hybrid only. No result-based threshold selection.
